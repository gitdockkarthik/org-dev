63. The Z3 failure of 9 Oct (21 incidents "closed since the old closer was switched off without a notice of their own") was a false alarm caused by two flaws of the audit, not by the system. The 21 were created and resolved on 8 and 9 Sep, were touched at 06:20:12 on 8 Oct (the live run of the history correction, which stamps updated_at by design), and each has a Closed-notice row with its own problem ID. The audit (1) dated a closure by the row's updated_at, so a September closure that a correction touched looked like a closure of 8 Oct, and (2) took a notice's time only from the alert history, which keeps 30 days: the notices of 8 Sep were still in it when the correction ran and had aged out a day later, so the audit saw no notice. Fixed by a80cb39: Z3, Z4 and Z7 date a closure by resolved_at; a notice that exists as a row but is older than the alert history is an INFO line ("notice older than the alert history, its time cannot be checked"), not "no notice". A closure after the switch-off with no notice row at all still fails Z3. Result: Z1 to Z7 PASS, 5,406 fully consistent, 1 unverifiable, 21 aged out (before 05:39) and 0 since, 8 without a notice of their own (the o1conf ones), 0 off. The lesson: any script that changes data stamps updated_at, so updated_at is not the date of a closure.
